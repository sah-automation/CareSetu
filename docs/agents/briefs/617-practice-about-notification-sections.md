# Brief - 617 Build the practice, about and notification sections

**Ticket:** #617 · **Parent:** #599 · **Refreshed:** 2026-09-30
**Reading surface:** ~8.7K tokens (budget 10K) - within budget

## Scope

The remaining sections stop being placeholders for structured data. Specialties, languages and consulting days become toggle groups the doctor taps, so no language can be recorded twice in two spellings and no day has to be formatted into a sentence. Consulting hours stay prose. Notification toggles become switches. And an accessibility scan runs inside the page suite - this is where the toggle groups and the switch are actually checked for keyboard and screen-reader correctness.

Acceptance criteria (from ticket):

- [ ] Specialties, languages and consulting days render as toggle groups that round-trip a selection, and consulting hours render as free prose.
- [ ] Notification preferences render as switches rather than bare native checkboxes.
- [ ] Each section saves independently, and a save's own reply never discards keystrokes in another section's buffer.
- [ ] An accessibility scan runs inside the page suite, following the pattern of the existing frontend suites that render locally and assert no violations, and introduces no new shared helper.
- [ ] Any pre-existing violation the scan finds is fixed in this change rather than deferred.
- [ ] Every new string ships in both locales and the bilingual parity suite passes.

## Handoff notes

- **Three blockers, and what they will have landed.** The ticket body names them positionally; resolved against the parent's child list they are **#605** (the section shell and edit buffer), **#608** (the practice section write) and **#610** (the about and notification section writes). Also transitively landed before you start: **#600** (the `Switch` and `ToggleGroup` primitives), **#602** (the closed vocabularies), **#606** (the structured profile columns, the multi-valued specialty and consulting-days storage, the reshaped languages column, and the removal of latitude/longitude from every update schema), and **#609** (the address write). Read #608's brief first if you have not - it is the in-batch template all three writes were written to, and its closing comment records how the route was registered so the next three could copy it.
- **Judgement call, recorded: the seed key is the whole answer object, and that is what makes AC 3 true.** The obvious implementation - each section's buffer seeding from its own slice of the projection - is wrong, and the failure is invisible until it bites: a save from one section returns a new whole projection, the source emits it, and the other two sections see a new object and reseed, discarding exactly the keystrokes AC 3 forbids losing. #605's brief makes the same call and records why: identity alone decides, and the guard must read the answer object, never the slice. Read its three guards in order; the order is part of the contract, not incidental.
- **Judgement call, recorded: the axe scan lives in the page suite, inline, with no new shared helper.** AC 4 says so explicitly, and there is a reason: eight suites already do this the same way, so a shared helper would be a ninth pattern's worth of invention for no gain. Scan a **container**, not the document - that is what the existing suites do and it is what keeps the scan scoped to the page's own markup. **Add one case per rendered state, and make sure at least one case has a non-empty selection:** a toggle group with nothing selected has far less to check than one with a pressed item, and the switch in its off state is much less interesting than the checked one. Assert `violations` to equal `[]`, exactly as the pattern does.
- **AC 5 is a real instruction, not a formality: fix what the scan finds.** Expect it to surface in the markup around the section headings and the field help, and possibly a labelled-control or `aria-describedby` gap in the existing read-only credential card next to your new sections. Fix it by adding the label, the description or the role - not by relaxing the rule, not by scoping the scan down, and not by adding an exclusion. If a violation genuinely cannot be fixed inside this ticket, that is a fact worth escalating, not something to hide from the assertion.
- **There is no `user-event` and this ticket does not add one.** It is not a dependency, the whole frontend suite is written against `fireEvent`, and the primitives' keyboard operability is #600's assertion - but AC 1 and AC 2 are about _this_ page's round-trip. Drive the keyboard path with `fireEvent.keyDown` / `fireEvent.keyUp` and assert the **observable** outcome (`aria-pressed` on the toggle item, the switch's `role` and checked state, or the Radix `data-state`), never internal state. Adding a devDependency the ticket did not ask for is the easy wrong turn here.
- **Assert the accessible role, not the visual control.** `Switch` renders a button with `role="switch"`; the thing it replaces is an `<input type="checkbox">`. The assertion that proves AC 2 is a role assertion, because that is what a screen reader actually announces - `getByRole("switch", { name: <the key's label> })`. For the toggle groups, `aria-pressed` per item is the assertion, plus the round-trip: select, save, and assert the rendered selection comes back from the read-back projection. AC 1 says "round-trip a selection", so a test that only asserts the toggle responds to a click has not proved it.
- **The five notification keys are the client-visible set, and the seeding rules are deliberate - keep them.** `seedNotifications` drops a stored key longer than the validator's cap because keeping it would make **every later save** 422, and seeds an absent key **off** because the doctor never chose. Both are judgements, not oversights, and #610's server-side merge rule is the other half of the same guarantee. The switch changes the control, not the semantics: the row is still one key, still off by default, still carries the `profile-notification-<key>` identity the existing suite reaches for. Do not "tidy" the seeding while you are converting checkboxes to switches.
- **The language de-dupe moves from client to server, but keep the convenience.** `splitLanguages` exists because the backend rejected duplicate names case-insensitively, so it folded the doctor's typing rather than failing their save over a double-tap. #610's closed vocabulary plus its merge rule is now the real guarantee. Keep the fold anyway - it stops the doctor producing a 422 with their own fingers - but do not treat it as the guarantee and do not let a comment claim the client is enforcing uniqueness. A selection drawn from a closed pick-list cannot produce two spellings at all, which is the ticket's actual motivation.
- **`experience_years` belongs to the practice section, not the about section, and its bound is #606's.** The tightened realistic range is landed by #606; the `0..100` check you can still see in the tree is the old one. Do not add experience years to the about mapper and do not validate them in the wrong section. Likewise, do not add a notification key that is not one of the five.
- **Consulting hours stay prose, and that is a decision, not an omission.** The days become a closed multi-select; the hours do not, and no `consulting_hours` parser belongs in this ticket. Keep the hours as free text with its existing length bound and let the backend keep it as prose.
- **The five copy-pasted chip groups elsewhere in the tree are out of scope even though they are the obvious next consumer.** `DirectoryBrowser`, the search card, the language toggle, a shared chip component and two other pages each carry their own `aria-pressed` group and their own `chipClass` factory. Consolidating them onto `ToggleGroup` is downstream work that this ticket does not own and should not start - the doctor profile page has no chip group today, so nothing here requires touching them. #600's brief and #605's brief both flag them as downstream; if you finish with time, note the consolidation as a follow-up rather than doing it.
- **Vocabulary drift is the easy mistake in this ticket.** The glossary's `specialty` entry carries an explicit `_Avoid_` list naming "consultation type" and "expertise", and the ticket body itself names both - they are dropped PRD phrases, not fields. Do not introduce either as a field, a label or a key.
